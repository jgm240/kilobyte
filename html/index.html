What it is

Kilobyte is a small Linux system, made from Debian, whose desktop is drawn entirely with coloured characters: box-drawing lines, block characters and shadows, like the text-mode programs of the 1980s. It has no graphics server and no toolkit: shell scripts on top of dialog and classic text-mode programs, plus a few small Python programs for games, Paint, the screen saver and the movable windows of the Kilobyte Desktop.

It starts in seconds, runs in 50 MB of memory, and is at home on old PCs, a Raspberry Pi 1, a laptop that only starts from CDs, and an e-ink Kindle. You are probably reading this in its web browser right now.

Programs

  • Work: Editor, Spreadsheet (also DOCX, ODT, XLSX, ODS), Email, Web browser, File manager, Terminal, Notes, Agenda, Cardfile, Calculator, Print
  • Internet: News feeds, Online videos (watch, save, or read what is said), Internet Time Machine (the web as it was), Chat, Messenger (Kilobytes on the same network, no account), BBS dialer, Host your own BBS, Network drives, Remote Kilobyte, Company desktops
  • Every day: Today (weather, appointments, headlines), Dictionary and translator, Encyclopedia (Wikipedia without the Internet), Sync between your computers, ASCII Art Generator, encrypted Backups (any file back from any day), Typing tutor, Fan control
  • Media: Video player (as coloured blocks, or the real picture), Music player, Podcasts, Pictures, Camera (with QR codes), DVDs and CDs, Paint
  • For everyone: a screen reader and braille displays (Settings > Accessibility; S in the boot menu speaks from the start), large letters, high contrast, your own colour themes, and kbutils to change settings in the Terminal
  • Games: Chess, Sudoku, text adventures, Mines, Solitaire, Reversi, Moon Buggy, Snake, Robots, Hangman, Adventure
  • Old programs: DOS programs and games (DOSBox), Windows programs (Wine, in a window), graphical Linux programs in a window (Open program in X), and a Store of text-mode programs from Debian

Runs on

  • PCs: 64-bit and 32-bit, from the Pentium 4 on: ISO and USB stick image; a CD version for PCs from the 1990s
  • Raspberry Pi: every model, from the Pi 1 and Zero to the Pi 5
  • Kindle: KilobytEink, for jailbroken Kindles
  • Your computer: next to your system: Linux (APT), macOS (Homebrew) and Windows (WinGet)

The Kilobyte Test Computers: MacBook Pro Late 2008, Medion Akoya E6240, and Sony VAIO PCG-71811M (photo).

Kilobyte has ten colour themes: Classic Blue, Blue Panels, Cyan Desk, Light Blue (8-bit), Blue and Orange (16-bit), Black and White (1984), Green Screen, Amber Screen, Ketchup and Mustard, and E-ink.
